Case 01 — Flagship · AI × Industrial engineering

Company Evaluation & Ranking System

An undergraduate thesis that refused to stay on a shelf. It now runs at Tehran Municipality.

Status: In operation

Role
Design and build — sole developer
Built at
Petro System
Period
1403–1404 on the Iranian calendar (2024–26)
Operated by
Tehran Municipality
Concepts
Web crawling · Information extraction · AI agents · KPIs · SMART

1Problem

Evaluating knowledge-based companies means reading scattered information about a lot of firms and comparing them fairly. By hand, that's slow, it depends on the evaluator's memory and taste, and nobody can say exactly why one company ranks above another.

2Bottleneck

Getting from scattered information to a score you can defend.

3Thinking

Decision model first, tools second. I wrote the problem down as an industrial-engineering problem: what should be measured, how each indicator is measured, how the scores combine. That became 5 main indicators and 50 sub-indicators, scored with the SMART multi-criteria approach. AI automates the collecting and the reading; the decision logic stays transparent and reviewable.

4System

System structure only. Indicator names and data belong to the client and aren't published here.

5Build

I designed and built the whole path, from the crawler to the scoring logic. The output isn't just a number: for every score, the system explains why it was given — because a decision nobody can explain doesn't survive inside an organisation.

6Where it stands

In operation at Tehran Municipality. Academic problem → engineered system → real-world use

Case 02 — A way of working · AI × Process

AI Business Process Automation

Agents, RAG and n8n workflows for the work that repeats itself every week.

Status: Ongoing

Role
Workflow design and build
Areas
Document generation · Proposal generation · Lead handling · Information extraction · Data pipelines
Tools
n8n · RAG · LLMs · Python · Claude

1Problem

In a lot of organisations, specialists spend a large share of their week on work with a fixed pattern: read a document, pull out a few numbers, write a draft, chase a lead. None of it is hard. All of it repeats.

2Bottleneck

Specialists stuck doing non-specialist work.

3Thinking

I treat every workflow like a production line: input, stations, an inspection point, output. AI sits where reading and writing happen. Where a mistake is expensive, a person signs off — and that checkpoint is designed in from day one, not bolted on after the first error.

4System

The maturity assessment is at the concept and architecture stage; no claim of a finished implementation is made.

5Build

A real one: a product-listing pipeline for a retailer's inventory — background removal, logo overlay, SEO copy and upload to the site, built with n8n, an image model and Python. The smaller tools in this family are listed in the Archive.

6Where it stands

Each workflow was built for one specific process. The maturity assessment is still concept and architecture.

Case 03 — Design × Front-end architecture

PARNIAN — a luxury cosmetics website

Brand idea: “Beauty is our red line.” Persian and right-to-left from the ground up, not flipped.

Status: Design study

Role
Design, architecture and build
Tools
Next.js 16 · React 19 · TypeScript · Tailwind v4
Runtime deps
Three
Output
Static export, GitHub Pages

1Problem

A lot of Persian websites are English templates held up to a mirror. The Persian typography suffers, letter-spacing breaks the joins between letters, and whatever luxury the brand had disappears right there.

2Bottleneck

RTL treated as a patch instead of as architecture.

3Thinking

Direction is a variable, not a fixed side: every layout rule is written with CSS logical properties, so an English locale is a one-line change. No Persian letter ever gets letter-spacing. Every colour, size, duration and easing curve is defined once, in a single token file.

4System

Moving to a CMS means swapping the data source, not rewriting components.

5Build

Home, collection, product detail, gallery, about and contact. The signature detail: each section quietly takes on the shade of the product you're reading about — one CSS custom property, no library, no per-frame work.

6Where it stands

A portfolio design study. No cart, no checkout, placeholder content.

Case 04 — Design × Luxury commerce

ZARNEGAR — a handmade gold & jewellery atelier

The second site, from the first system: PARNIAN's architecture, an entirely different identity.

Status: Design study

Role
Design and build
Content
Nine pieces in three categories, a gallery, a brand story
Purchase path
One inquiry path; no prices, no cart
Tools
Next.js 16 · TypeScript · Playwright

1Problem

Handmade jewellery doesn't sell from a price table. Before the first message, a buyer has to trust the work: the detail, the maker's hand, the fact that the piece won't be made twice.

2Bottleneck

Trust, before the first message.

3Thinking

No prices, no cart — one path to an inquiry. The visual hierarchy walks the eye from the piece to its detail and then to a conversation. Conversion here means starting a conversation, not clicking “buy”.

4System

The template's rule: every derived brand gets exactly one signature element taken from what that trade actually does. For a jeweller, it's an engraving preview — the buyer types their own line, watches it cut into the plate, and that same text goes into the inquiry.

5Build

Derived from the PARNIAN template: same content contract, same tokens, new identity and content. Smoke-tested with Playwright against the static export.

6Where it stands

A design study. The brand is fictional and the photographs are generated placeholders.

Case 05 — Design × Traditional industry

Keyhan Nema Ilya — a cardboard & packaging manufacturer

Translating a traditional B2B manufacturer into a modern digital experience — without sanding off what makes it industrial.

Status: Demo

Business
Cardboard and packaging manufacturing, B2B
Role
Design and build
Tools
Next.js 15 · TypeScript · Tailwind v4 · GSAP · Lenis

1Problem

A traditional cardboard and packaging factory. The product's real value — a five-layer structure that absorbs impact — is invisible in a photo of a closed box.

2Bottleneck

Quality that can't be seen can't be sold.

3Thinking

Don't claim it; show the structure. Use the drawing an engineer would make to explain a part: an exploded view. As you scroll, the carton comes apart layer by layer — printed face, board, flute, board, inner liner — and each layer explains its job.

4System

One photograph is cut into five clipped layers that separate on scroll. Drag the slider to see it.

5Build

A scroll-driven motion system with GSAP and Lenis, a custom cursor, and a product gallery.

6Where it stands

Demo. The trust cards are deliberately built with explicit blanks so the real figures come from the factory itself. I don't put invented numbers on a client's site.

Case 06 — Trading × AI × Product

TradeWithBardia — a systematic trading research ecosystem

An academy, a setup library, a journal — and a tool that puts Claude in front of FX Replay.

Status: In progress

Role
All of it: strategy, code, design, content
Parts
TradeWithBardia · JournalWithBardia · FXR Pilot · AIwithBardia
Tools
Next.js · TypeScript · Python · Playwright · MCP · Claude Code

1Problem

I've written 30+ mechanical setups, each with a target tier, an acceptance bar and a minimum sample size. But an untested setup is only a hypothesis — and manual backtesting moves one candle, one trade, one screenshot at a time.

2Bottleneck

Manual backtesting: the slowest station on the whole research line.

3Thinking

Build the measuring instrument before making any claim. And one rule above all others: no fabricated trading data. Every chart declares where its data came from, and until a real sample exists, no win rate or equity curve gets shown.

4System

How an outcome is decided
Default target2R
A candle that reaches both target and stopStop
On the fill candleA stop touch counts; a target touch doesn't
Candle prices can't be readStop stepping, so no hit is ever missed

The rules are pessimistic on purpose: every ambiguity is counted against the system.

Ecosystem
  • TradeWithBardiaBilingual site: academy, setups, dictionary, systemsLive · Phase 0
  • JournalWithBardiaIn-browser journal: record the decision, review it, classify it; every figure computed from the recordsDemo
  • FXR PilotMCP server for Claude-driven backtesting on FX ReplayPhase 1 done
  • AIwithBardiaAI for tradersIdea

5Build

The site: Next.js as a static export, bilingual through CSS logical properties, with a nine-beat scroll-driven hero — market, noise, structure, liquidity, sweep, MSS, FVG, execution, system. Scenes are data: if a scene's story stops matching its candles, the build fails. FXR Pilot: Python, Playwright and MCP, tested end-to-end against a mock FX Replay page, with a mechanical ICT engine (sweep → market structure shift → FVG entry).

6Where it stands

The site is live at Phase 0 and the journal runs as a demo. FXR Pilot has finished phase one; calibration on the real FX Replay and the backtests themselves come next. Until those samples exist, no statistics get published.

DelaySheet 4 of 6

Most of trading is waiting.

Trading is my lab for deciding under uncertainty. Every day the market asks the same question any system asks: when you can't know how a single decision will turn out, which rule do you trust?

My answer: define the risk before you enter, keep the target fixed, and leave judgement to a big enough sample — never to the last trade. The hard half is the waiting: for the setup, and for the sample to grow.

Got a slow process?

Tell me in three sentences: what the work is, how often it happens, and where it gets stuck. We'll start there.

I don't replace your specialists. I shorten the work that eats days of their time.

Bottlenecks I see a lot

  • A report rebuilt by hand every month, for every company in a group.
  • Choosing among many options or proposals with no written criteria.
  • Reviewing documents and proposals that takes days.
  • Numbers that don't agree from one report to the next.
  • Work that repeats every week and is done by hand every time.
